**"Ordinary" Listening**
- William Ury defines ordinary listening as an act of hearing the words and thinking: "Where do *I* agree?", "Where do *I* disagree?", "What do I *say*?"
- This thinking puts the spotlight on the listener instead of the speaker. 
	- "The focus is on **us**" 

**Genuine Listening**
- "In genuine listening, we listen for not just what's said, but what's not being said."
- In genuine listening, you put yourself in the shoes of the listener, and put the spotlight and focus on them. This includes:
	- Listening for underlying emotions & feelings.
	- Listening for the underlying needs of the speaker.
	- Listen for the deeper meaning of their words.
- In William Ury's example, he highlights that listening is about listening to the *person*, not the words. 
- Making them feel seen, heard, and truly **trying to understand** what they wanted to say.

**Why Is It So Hard to Listen?**
- "If listening is so useful, why isn't everybody doing it?"
- William Ury highlights that listening is difficult because there is a *lot* going on in our minds. This makes us not have the emotional and mental capacity to carry other people's burdens. 
- But Ury also offers an unorthodox solution. He finds that what helps to clear one's mind so they can listen to other's is **listening to yourself.** 
- Taking a quiet moment to feel what you feel, and think what you think. Observe not only what's around you, but within you. 
- This process helps you let go of your own feelings before you take on someone else's.
- William Ury believes that listening to yourself first makes it easier to listen to others. 

**The Power of Listening**
- William Ury believes that listening will help immensely by pacifying and preventing conflicts before they even start. 
- He calls today's age an age of communication, which he dreams will become an age of listening. He calls this a **listening revolution.**
- He desires for listening to become the norm amongst all areas of life. School, work, governance, business, media, etc. 
- He believes that a listening revolution, while not removing all conflict, may avert many unnecessary fights and wars and conflicts. 

**The Call to Action**
- He characterizes the act of listening as **contagious.** And that it is a chain reaction started by somebody. William Ury calls the audience to act on this and become the catalyst for the chain reaction. Very inspiring!
