**Listening**
- It is a skill of Language
- A combination of physical hearing and psychological involvement
- Requires a desire to understand, an attitude of respect and acceptance, and a willingness to open one's mind to try to see things from another's point of view.
- Requires high level of concentration and energy. To listen, we need to set aside our own agendas and put ourselves in others' shoes. 

**What is Listening? (In technical terms):**
- Listening is one of 4 language modalities (listening, speaking, reading & writing). 
- Involved **active involement**
- Has a sender, a message, and receiver. 
- Psychological process of:
	- Receiving messages (verbal/non-verbal)
	- Constructing meaning from messages (verbal/non-verbal)
	- Responding to messages  (verbal/non-verbal)

**Key Components of Listening:**
-  Discriminating between sounds
	- Recognizing words 
	- Understanding the meaning of words 
	- Identifying grammatical groupings of words
	- Identifying expressions and sets of utterances that create meaning (grunts, non-verbal things, or sayings )
	- Connecting [[Linguistic, Non-Linguistic, and Paralinguistic Cues]].
	- Using background knowledge (+context) to predict and confirm meaning 
	- Recalling important words and ideas.

**Process of Listening:**
- **Hearing**
	- Response caused by sound waves stimulating the sensory receptors of the ear. 
	- The physical response of percepting sound waves. 
	- **You must hear to listen, but you need not listen to hear.**
	- Selective perception (hearing but not listening) is the result of a lack of ***attention***
- **Understanding**
	- Analyzing the meaning of stimuli we perceive. 
	- Recognize **symbols** (which aren't just words, but also non-verbal ways of communication and even outfits) that contextualize speech.
	- **Understanding** is recognizing the meaning and context assumed by the sender.
- **Remembering**
	- Adding gathered information into the "mind's storage bank"
	- Memory can be just as selective as attention. What we remember from a conversation may be quite different from what was originally seen or heard. 
- **Evaluating**
	- Requires active listening to get to this stage. 
	- The process of weighing evidence, sorting facts from opinions, and determining the presence/absence of bias or prejudice in a message. 
	- Only begins after all *necessary* information is gathered through the listening process. 
- **Responding**
	- Completing the process through verbal/non-verbal **feedback**. 
	- Because the initial speaker has no other way to determine if a message has been received, **responding** is the only way for the sender to determine the degree of success in transmitting the message. 

**Strategies of Listening**
Techniques or activities that contribute directly to the comprehension and recollection of listening input. There are different classifications of listening strategies, sorted by **how the listener processes the input.**

1. **Top-down Strategies.** Listener based. Relies on the listener to know background knowledge about the topic, situation, context, type of text, and language. (ex. predictions, inferences, summarizing.)
2. **Bottom-up Strategies.** Speaker based. Relies on the speaker's language and tone which indicates meaning. (ex. listening for specific details, recognizing patterns, etc.)

**Three Basic Modes of Listening**

**Active/Reflective Listening**
- Most useful and important listening strategy. 
- Requires the listener to be genuinely interested in understanding what the speaker is thinking, feeling, or wanting to say. 
- Often uses strategies such as paraphrasing and restatement to provide feedback to the sender. 
- The feedback (or verification) process is what makes active listening distinguished and effective. (Also why it's called "Reflective" listening)
- The **Essentials of Active Listening** are: 
	1. Intensity 
	2. Empathy 
	3. Acceptance
	4. Recognizing Responsibility for completeness.

**Passive/Attentive Listening**
- Like active listening, requires the listener to genuinely be engaged in hearing and understanding the sender's point of view and message. 
- No reflection/verification/feedback. 

**Competitive/Combative Listening**
- Happens when the listener is more interested in promoting his own point of view rather than understanding or exploring the sender's view. 
- Listeners with this type of listening mode look for openings to take the floor, or actively looks for weaknesses in the sender's message.

**Types of Listening**
Based on the objective of the listener and manner in which he responds to the message. 

1. **Active listening** - Demonstrates interest and encourages continued speaking.
2. **Appreciative listening** - Seeks to show appreciation. Can also mean listening for pleasure/entertainment. (As in- you appreciate what you listen to, such as music.)
3. **Attentive listening** - Seeks to show obvious attention. (Nodding head, eye contact, etc.)
4. **Biased listening** - Filters message with personal bias. 
5. **Casual listening** - Doesn't seek to validate speaker, and actual attention can vary. 
6. **Comprehension listening** - Seeks to find meaning. 
7. **Critical listening** - Seeks to evaluate, criticize, or pass judgement on the message. 
8. **Deep listening** - Seeks to understand sender's personality through not just their message, but other unspoken meanings and motivations.
9. **Discriminative listening** - Seeks to find a specific sound/message.
10. **Empathetic listening** - Seeks to understand what the sender is feeling while actively demonstrating it. 
11. **Evaluative listening** - Same as **Critical listening** (for some reason?)
12. **Inactive listening** - Seeks to deceive the sender by pretending to listen. 
13. **Judgemental listening** - Same as **Critical listening** (and evaluative listening, for some reason??)
14. **Partial listening** - Seeks to listen fully, but distractions and other cognitive functions occupy the listener's mind. 
15. **Reflective listening** - Seeks to paraphrase or reiterate the message sent as a form of feedback/verification. 
16. **Relationship listening** - Seeks to develop a relationship with the sender.
17. **Sympathetic listening** - Listening with concern for the well-being of the speaker. (Not sure if this seeks to actively display this.)
18. **Therapeutic Listening** - Seeks to understand what the sender is feeling, actively displaying empathy 
19. **Total listening** - Seeks to purely focus on the message and it's deeper meaning, contextualized by the tone of the speaker.

**The Importance of the Listening Skill**
- Good listening skills produce productivity. It allows a person to:
	- Understand assignments better, implicitly knowing what is expected from them. 
	- Build rapport with others, show support for them, and provide a better team-based environment. 
	- Resolve problems through understanding, asking, and answering questions.
	- Find the underlying meaning of what others say through tone, choice of words, and other parts of speech. 

**Ways to Improve Listening Skills**
1. **Face the speaker.** 
	- Show your attentiveness through body language (sitting up straight or leaning back, eye contact, nodding, etc.)
	- **Minimize internal and external distractions.** 
	- **Respond appropriately** to show that you understand. Combine verbal and non-verbal cues to send feedback to the sender on whether their message is succeeding transmission or not.
	- **Keep an open mind** to all topics. Hear and process all information first before deciding to agree or disagree. 
	- **Listen before you talk.** Organize your ideas before you speak out or ask a question. Allow the speaker to make their point before you do yours. 

2. **Engage.** Ask questions after the speaker is done talking, reflect their message, paraphrase, and show them that you are listening. 

**Barriers to Listening**
1. **Physiological Barriers.** Hard of hearing, deafness, blindness, mental illness, disorders, etc. 
2. **Physical Barriers.** Environmental distractions such as a wall, smoke, external sounds, heat, etc. Over stimulation of the senses also count as physical barriers.
3. **Attitudinal Barriers** - Distractions related to the listener's mindset and/or personal struggles. These can be controlled and, through discipline, overcome. (ex. egocentrism, thinking about other things more important than the conversation, etc.)
4. **Wrong Assumptions** - The assumption that the listener is always listening, or assuming that the speaker knows about what he/she is saying. The assumptions which lead to misunderstanding and lack of clarification. 
5. **Cultural Barriers** - Differing backgrounds, vocal accents, and overall interpretation of the world around speaker and a listener. 
6. **Lack of Training** - People are not born good listeners. It is developed through practice and training. 

**Benefits of Effective Listening Skills**
- **Effective communication**
- **Fewer Misunderstandings**
- **Improved Relationships**
- **Personal Growth**

**Common Listening Mistakes**
- Interrupting the speaker
- Completing the speaker's messages/sentence in advance. 

**A very important component of listening is *letting the speaker talk,* you will have your time to provide feedback.**
