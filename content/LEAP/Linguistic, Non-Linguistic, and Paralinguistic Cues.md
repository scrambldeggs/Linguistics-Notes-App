**Linguistic cues** are specific features of spoken or written language—such as tone, word choice, grammar, and rhythm—that help people understand the underlying meaning, intent, or context of a message. They act as mental signposts guiding how listeners or readers interpret communication.

- **Semantic Cues:** Meaning-based clues from specific words, vocabulary, or labels that activate background knowledge.
- **Syntactic Cues:** Structural clues based on grammar, word order, and parts of speech that show how words fit together in a sentence.
- **Prosodic Cues:** Vocal features like pitch, stress, rhythm, and intonation patterns that change or emphasize spoken meaning.
- **Pragmatic/Contextual Cues:** Social or situational choices in language code and style that signal politeness, irony, or social standing.
---
**Non-linguistic cues** are completely non-verbal and non-vocal. They rely entirely on body language, physical space, and visual signals to convey meaning.

- **Kinesics:** Body movements, including facial expressions, eye contact, and hand gestures.
- **Proxemics:** Physical distance between speakers, which signals levels of intimacy or authority.
- **Haptics:** Communication through touch, such as handshakes, pats on the back, or hugs.
- **Posture:** Body positioning like open vs. crossed arms, slouching, or standing tall.
- **Artifacts:** Physical appearance, clothing choices, and environment that signal status or intent.
---
Paralinguistics refer to the vocal but non-verbal elements of speech. They modify the meaning of your spoken words but do not rely on the vocabulary itself.

- **Pitch:** High or low vocal tones that signal excitement, anger, or questioning.
- **Volume:** Loudness or softness indicating confidence, aggression, or secrecy.
- **Tempo:** Speech rate where fast talking implies nervousness and slow talking implies deliberation.
- **Tone:** Emotional coloring of the voice like sarcasm, warmth, or hostility.
- **Pauses:** Silences or filler words ("um," "ah") that indicate hesitation, emphasis, or a change of thought.
