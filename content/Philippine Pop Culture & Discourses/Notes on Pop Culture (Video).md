What is culture? 
- Etymologically - Comes from French and means 'to culture', or 'to grow'. 

Primitive Culture (1871) - Sir Edward Burnett Tylor
- Culture, as defined by Tyler is "That complex whole which includes knowledge, beliefs, arts, morals, laws, customs, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by a member of society."
- The starting point of figuring out the boundaries of culture. 

Raymond Williams 
- Says "culture" is one of the most complex words in the english Language. 
- Wrote Culture & Society 1780-1950 (1950)
- Wrote Keywords: A Vocabulary of Culture & Society (1976)
- According to Williams, in Keywords  (1976), culture is:
	- a general process of intellectual, spiritual and aesthetic development
	- a particular way of life, whether of a people, a period or a group
	- the works and practices of intellectual and especially artistic activity (signifying practices)

Matthew Arnold & F.R. Leavis 
- Culture is not a "way of life" or anything broad. 
- For them, "Culture" was the finer things. Elite things in order. For them, "Mass culture" was harmful and discordant. 

**So what *is* Culture?**
- A learned, shared behavior
- It's, at once, individual and necessarily and inherently social. 
- Culture requires continuous practice. Culture is *done* by people. 
- Culture is the stylization of life. 
- It's cultivated, one isn't born with culture, and one absorbs, produces, and reproduces it. Like mutating crops. 

**Pop Culture**
- Cultural Theory and Popular Culture by John Storey views it as a few different things:
	- "Widely favored/liked" or quantitative dimension
	- Leftovers of "high" culture, somehow inferior to it due to being watered down or disorganized
	- Mass produced (mass culture), commercial, formulaic
	- Culture that emerges from "the people (the masses, not every person)" (vox populi)
	- Site of ideological struggle... dominant/subordinate people and forces
	- Perhaps culture is one thing, and there is no high/low culture.
- Henry Glassie
	- He argues: Pop culture varies maximally through time but minimally through space.
	- While folk/high culture varies minimally through time but maximally through space.
	- **What does this mean?**
		- An object of Pop culture doesn't change in the context of space. 
		- Yet an object of pop culture does change many, many times overtime. 
		- As for folk/high culture, it is valued inherently due to its prestige. So it doesn't allow itself to change through that metric.
		- Yet for different places, what is considered "elite/high" in culture varies significantly.
