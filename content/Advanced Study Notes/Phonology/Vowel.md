In [[linguistics]], a vowel is a speech sound produced with an open vocal tract so that air flows freely from the lungs without major blockages or friction. **It acts as the core (nucleus) of a syllable.** 

In this sense, vowels are essentially the opposites of consonants, with [[Manner of Articulation#**Approximant**|approximants]] being a kind of half-vowel half-consonant because they are produced the same way as a vowel but cannot be the nucleus of a syllable.

Within the English language, all vowels are [[Consonant#**Voiced & Voiceless Consonants**|voiced]]. (With the exception of whispering.)

![[IPA Vowel Trapezoid Chart.png]]

The Vowel IPA chart describes the position of the tongue and shape of the lips as you sound out vowels. The top, bottom, front, and back of the chart represent the same positions within your mouth. 

The terms on this chart describe the **tongue elevation** (vertical axis) and the **tongue position** (horizontal axis). Whereas "roundedness" describe the shape of the lips as you sound out a vowel.

The 5 vowels that exist within English are:
- /a/ - The tongue is placed very low (Open tongue elevation) on the mouth, opening a wide path for the air from the lungs to come through (this also requires the jaw to come down). The tongue is also placed in a neutral, relaxed position between the front and the back of the mouth (Central tongue position.) This vowel is unrounded, meaning that the lips sit in a neutral shape and don't nearly touch each other.
