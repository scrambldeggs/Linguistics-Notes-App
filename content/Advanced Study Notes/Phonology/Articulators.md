Articulators are the physiological organs in the mouth that allow us to... well, articulate and create sounds. 

It's official definition states: 

In phonetics, articulators are the physiological organs in the vocal tract that move or shape the airflow from the lungs into recognizable speech sounds.

There are two kinds of Articulators:
- **Active Articulators** - the organs that can move (e.g. Tongue, Lower Lip, Velum, Uvula, Glottis)
- **Passive Articulators** - the stationary organs (e.g. Upper Lip, Upper Teeth, Alveolar Ridge, Hard Palate, Velum or Soft Palate, Pharynx) that active articulators move towards and interact with in order to direct the flow of air. 

-> Notice that the Velum (Soft Palate) is both an active & passive articulator. This is because other articulators can move *towards it* to create sounds, making it passive. And on it's own, it can move up or down to direct airflow (either towards the mouth, or the nose), making it active.
